How to playThe aim, the setup and how every hole is scored

Get up and down. Every hole is par 2: one short-game shot and one putt.

Setup

  1. Answer the three questions about your practice area: how far you can chip, whether you have a bunker, and the longest bunker shot you can play.
  2. Choose the level. Easy · Medium · Hard is the standard game. Easy · Medium · Medium is for less advanced players.
  3. Enter your name, pick a target handicap and tap Start round.

The game

  1. 9 holes, one ball per hole. The app tells you the shot for every hole: the lie, the distance and the situation. You don't get to choose.
  2. Set the ball up as described and play it out until it's holed.
  3. If you like, enter how far the first shot finished from the hole. Then tap your score for the hole.
  4. If your practice area can't give you the shot described, tap "Can't set this up" for another shot of the same difficulty.

Scoring

Strokes on a holeResult
1Chip-in, birdie
2Up and down, par
3Bogey
4 or moreDouble or worse
  • Par for the round is 18. Most good players will be over par, because the shots get harder.

Handicap and report

  • Every shot has an expected score for each handicap, so your handicap is worked out from the shots you actually got. A hard draw counts for more than an easy one.
  • Your Par 18 handicap is the average of your last 5 rounds, so it gets more accurate the more you play.
  • At the end, tap Send to coach to send the full round report.

Handicap guide

Expected 9-hole score for an average draw at each level, using your practice area settings.

How the handicap is worked out

Every shot type has a PGA Tour baseline, the average strokes to hole out from that distance and lie (Mark Broadie's strokes gained data: for example 2.40 from 20 yds on the fairway, 2.59 from the rough and 2.53 from a bunker). Harder situations such as short-sided, downslope or plugged lies add to that baseline.

Each handicap then loses a set amount per shot, scaled by how hard the shot is. A bunker or hard lie widens the gap more than a simple chip. The gaps are set so that up-and-down rates line up with Shot Scope's amateur data (about 54% inside 50 yds for scratch, 39% for a 10 handicap and 31% for a 20 handicap; sand saves of 37%, 20% and 15%).

Your round handicap compares your score with the expected score for the exact 9 shots you were given. One 9-hole round varies by several strokes, so a single round can be out by up to 8 shots of handicap. The 5-round average is the number to trust.
