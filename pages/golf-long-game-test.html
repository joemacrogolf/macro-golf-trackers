How it worksThe test, the scoring and what you get at the end

Every full-swing club, from 100 yds to driver, on a launch monitor. Tee shots use total distance and offline; approaches use carry and offline or distance from the pin. Every shot is scored by strokes gained against your target handicap.

Before you start

  1. Warm up fully. The full test takes about 75 minutes and the half test about 40. Your progress saves if you need to stop.
  2. Enter your driver carry and your longest fairway club carry, so the targets suit your game.

The test

  1. The app gives you the shot: an approach to a target, or a tee shot on a hole. Distances are random each time.
  2. Choose your club and hit one ball. Shape doesn't matter: only where it lands.
  3. Tee shots: enter total distance and offline. Approaches: carry and offline, or distance from the pin if you chose that at the start.
  4. Some shots have water, out of bounds or a fairway bunker on one side, shown on the picture of the hole. Missing big on the water or out-of-bounds side costs a penalty shot. Finishing in a fairway bunker leaves a harder next shot.

Scoring

  • Each shot is scored in strokes gained against your target handicap: plus means better than that handicap would expect from the same shot, minus means worse.
  • Longer shots have more room for error. Finishing 20 ft away from 180 yds is a much better shot than 20 ft from 110 yds.
  • On tee shots, distance and finding the fairway both count.
  • At the end you get a long game handicap, a rating for each club group, and your strengths and weaknesses.

How it's scored

Average strokes to hole out from the fairway at each level.

Where the numbers come from

The Tour baseline is Mark Broadie's strokes gained data: from the fairway a PGA Tour player averages 2.80 strokes to hole out from 100 yds, 2.98 from 160 yds and 3.19 from 200 yds, and 1.87 putts from 20 ft.

Each handicap adds a set amount per shot, more from further away and from the rough, so that a 20 handicap averages about 3.8 from 150 yds and a scratch player about 3.1. Those gaps were checked against Shot Scope's average proximity by handicap (about 38 ft for scratch, 57 ft for a 10 handicap and 81 ft for a 20 handicap from 100–150 yds), allowing for the perfect lie on a launch monitor. Tee shots use each level's typical driving distance and fairway rate (for example 270 yds and 55% for scratch, 215 yds and 42% for a 20 handicap).

Your shot's finish is worked out from the numbers you enter (total distance for tee shots). Approaches start from the fairway value for that distance. Within about 10–14 yds of the target counts as on the green (bigger allowance for longer shots), then the putt or chip that follows is valued from the same tables. Every shot's value is that expected score before the shot, minus one, minus the expected score after it.

These are estimates built on published averages, not an official handicap.
