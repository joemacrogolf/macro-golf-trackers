How to playThe set-up, the order and the scoring

24 putts from 3, 4 and 5 ft, from four sides of the hole. Make or miss, every putt scored in strokes gained.

Set up

  1. Pick a hole on a slope. North is directly above the hole, so that putt is downhill. South is directly below it, so that putt is uphill.
  2. East and West are the two sidehill spots, level with the hole: one breaks right to left, the other left to right.
  3. Mark 3, 4 and 5 ft at each of the four spots. Tees work well.

The game

  1. Work round the hole from 3 ft: North, East, South, West. Then the same from 4 ft, then 5 ft. That's 12 putts.
  2. Then go round all 12 again: 24 putts in total.
  3. One ball each, full routine. Tap Holed or Missed.

Scoring

  • Each putt is strokes gained against your target handicap. A holed putt gains a little; a miss costs a lot.
  • At the end you get a handicap for your short putting, your makes by length and by putt type, and your strengths and weaknesses.

Where the numbers come from

The Tour row is Mark Broadie's strokes gained putting data: tour players hole about 99% from 3 ft, 93% from 4 ft and 88% from 5 ft.

The handicap rows use Shot Scope make rates by handicap from short range.

A holed putt gains the chance your target level would have missed it; a miss costs the chance they'd have holed it, plus the tap-in that follows. These are estimates built on published averages, not an official handicap.
