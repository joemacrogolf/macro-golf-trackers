How to playThe aim, the setup and how every putt is scored

Hole as many of the 20 putts as you can. These are the lengths that win and lose matches.

The game

  1. 5 putts each from 6, 8, 10 and 12 ft, starting at 6 ft and working back.
  2. Every putt tells you what to set up: uphill, downhill or flat, and whether it is straight or a small, medium or high break, left to right or right to left. Find that putt on the green before you hit.
  3. Full routine on every putt, one ball at a time.
  4. Tap Made or Missed after each putt.
  5. If the green can't give you the putt described, tap "Can't set this up?" for a different one.

Handicap guide

Make rates on a flat, gentle-breaking putt, and the expected score out of 20 for an average set of putts.

How these numbers were worked out

PGA Tour make rates come from ShotLink: about 70% from 6 ft, 53% from 8 ft, 41% from 10 ft and 30% from 12 ft.

Amateur rates come from Shot Scope data by handicap. For example, from 6–9 ft scratch golfers hole 49%, 10 handicaps 39% and 20 handicaps 33%. From 9–12 ft it is 34%, 26% and 18%. Each exact distance was read off between those bands.

Every putt is judged on its length only: the make rate for that distance, whatever the slope or break. Your expected score is built from the exact 20 putts you were given.

20 putts still varies a lot from one session to the next, so judge the average over several sessions.
