How to playThe aim, the setup and how every tee shot is scored

Read the hole, pick the club and the line, then hit the shot. The sim numbers decide where it finishes.

Setup

  1. Warm up with 5 drives. These are not scored.
  2. Enter your name, your average driver carry and a target handicap. You are put on the tee box that gives you the best test.
  3. Set the sim to a straight range target so offline is measured from your start line.

The game

  1. 14 tee shots on 14 different holes. Some are long, some are short, every one has rough and most have trouble.
  2. Study the picture. The yellow band shows where your chosen club normally carries.
  3. Choose your club.
  4. Pick a target on the range that matches your plan, run your full routine and hit it.
  5. Enter the total distance and offline from the sim, choose left or right, and tap Score shot.

Scoring

  • Each shot is scored in strokes gained against a tour baseline for that hole length.
  • Fairway beats rough, rough beats sand, sand beats trees. Water costs a penalty drop. Out of bounds means re-teeing, hitting 3.
  • Fairway roll is added for you. Anything that rolls into a bunker or water stays there.

Handicap and report

  • The board shows your running strokes gained and the handicap you are driving like.
  • At the end, tap Email my coach to send the session report.

    How these numbers were worked out

    Every shot is scored with strokes gained: the expected strokes to hole out from the tee, minus one for the shot, minus the expected strokes from where the ball finished (plus any penalty). The expected-strokes values come from Mark Broadie's PGA Tour baseline for tee, fairway, rough, sand, recovery and green.

    The handicap guide uses published strokes-gained research on how much each handicap level loses off the tee to a tour player. Because you play from tees matched to your distance, the guide is an approximate guide, not an exact one. A sim gives perfect lies and no wind, so judge the average over several sessions.

    Tee boxes scale every hole's length and hazard distances to your carry. Fairway and hazard widths stay the same, as they do on a real course.
